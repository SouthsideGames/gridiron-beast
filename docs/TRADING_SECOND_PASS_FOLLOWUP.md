## 1. The 25-monster roster
**Where:** benchmark stage D, scenario `expiring`, seed 1, season 5, Sundown Drakes (Task 5 tree). The same scenario in v42.16.0 never went below 27 (seed 2).
**Trace** (`node tools/roster-trace.mjs --seed=1 --club="Sundown Drakes" --scenario=expiring --seasons=6`): 38 monsters through the season; at the season-end contract step 15 unprotected monsters **walked** as their deals expired (38 → 23); the club's offseason refill (free agency, `leagueFreeAgency`) and the draft then brought it back; the benchmark's season-end observation caught it at 25. At the start of season 6 the club had 38. No trade was involved: the whole drop is the contract-expiry step (`leagueContracts`), the same step that takes baseline clubs from 38 to 29 in the same scenario.
**Can the game enter a season illegally?** No. `startNextSeason` refuses an owner roster under 26 (checked by a test); for AI clubs the offseason runs free agency then `validateClubs` and `verifyLeague` (fatal if the registry is damaged) before the season starts. Experiment and regression test: three clubs cut to 25, 18 and 10 monsters in the offseason all start the next season at 38 with legal lineups and under the cap (`tests/trading-second-pass.test.js`, "roster floor").
**Did Task 5 introduce or worsen it?** No. It is a pre-existing, transient state of the offseason (between expiry and refill). Over 10,200 AI club-seasons at the season-end observation: baseline minimum 27, Task 5 minimum 25 (this one case); clubs at 28 or fewer: 27 baseline, 17 Task 5. Task 5's trades cannot cause it: a deal may not take a club below 26 (`rosterShort`), and offseason deals happen before the refill. The scenario synchronises the whole roster's contracts, so the cliff recurs four seasons later; in baseline the cohort had been broken up by other moves.
**Fix:** none needed; a regression test now guards the refill, the owner floor, and "AI sellers give up a spare, never a starter".

## 2. In-season trade quality (seeds 1–20, 10 seasons, 183 deals, ~0.9 a season; `audit-task5-seeds1-20.md`)
Measured by an instrument that does not use the market-value function: the game's own depth chart, plain ratings, payroll, picks, and contract years, on the rosters as they stood the week before each deal (a monster out 3+ weeks does not start; 1–2 week injuries do not change the starters, as in the game's rule).
- **Need and starter improvement.** The buyer's slot improves by 8 or more points in 91 deals (50%), by 4–8 in 88 (48%), by less in 4 (2%). Starters across the lineup improve by 8.2 points on average.
- **Cap.** Payroll moves by a median of +1,960 cr (0.1% of the cap), at most 5,880 (0.3%); no buyer ends below 5,400 cr room; four deals (2%) push a club under 1.5% of the cap.
- **Assets surrendered.** 134 deals pay a monster plus one pick, 49 are monster for monster. Picks: 13 first-rounders, 43 seconds, 78 thirds, none paying two. First-rounders went mostly on stars (for example a QB 77, +13 points, and an RB 78, +12) and once on a kicker (below).
- **Long term.** Mean age 26.2; one deal (0.5%) buys a 33+ monster (S 70, age 34, contract ending this year, +14 points); one acquired monster left within a season. Sellers' starters change by +0.5 on average; one deal (0.5%) cost a seller a starter point loss of 4 or more (a CB group with a long-injured star sold a healthy 76 for a 69 and a first-round pick).
- **Flags.** 11 of 183 (6%) trip any harm rule, 3 of them for no lineup gain (a depth swap that did not help a lineup). The rule set and its thresholds are written at the top of `tools/trade-audit.mjs` before the first run.
**Recommendation: keep the in-season bar (`AI_TRADE_RULES.season`: upgrade 4, minGain 16) as it is.** The decision quality is high (98% give a real lineup gain, none strip the seller, price and cap barely move), and the volume above baseline is the window that was effectively dead (0.03 a season) now working. The only lever for fewer deals is a higher bar; it would remove deals the audit finds good. No cap on volume is proposed, as instructed.
**Limit of the instrument.** Offseason deals happen after the ratings have aged since the last weekly check, so roster-level flags for offseason deals are inflated by stale ratings (e.g. 9% "hollow seller", 4% "no gain" in the report). Checked directly at the moment of each deal: in 120 offseason deals and in the new regression test the seller gave up a spare every time. Offseason results are therefore used only for deal-intrinsic facts (picks paid, kicker, ages).

**First-round picks for kickers:** 107 → 4 per 40 seeds in the benchmark; 21 kicker deals in the 20-seed audit, 3 with a first-round pick. Of those 3: seed 1 (K70, +9 points over the starter, in season), seed 9 (K65, club with no kicker), seed 10 (K77, +10). ## 4. Owner trading restrictions
**The 6-overall replacement gap (`owner-restriction-study.txt`, 890 tradable AI monsters, 3 franchises).** It applies only to the AI club's side: it is never applied to the owner's own monsters, who face only roster, cap, and the "only one at the position" legality rule. Measured as it stood: 70% of AI starters (96% of the best quartile; every QB) could not be bought for picks alone, and 0% of backups and bench, so **prospects and spares for picks were never held back** (prospects are not starters). Widening the gap to 8/10/12 would hold back 54/40/29%. **Defect found:** the check ignored what the owner sends back in the same proposal, so a like-for-like swap or an upgrade at a position was refused, and the desk would not even let the owner select the starter. 
**Change made (one clause, for owner review):** what the owner sends counts as the replacement (judged on the roster after the deal, for all starters asked together), and a starter without cover is selectable with a note. Effect: 81% of the tried like-for-like swaps now clear the rule (0% before), and 80% are accepted at the unchanged price; a prospect-for-starter swap clears in 19% (a bench prospect is not a replacement, correctly). The gap itself stays at 6, AI–AI clubs still sell only spares, no price check changed. This is a relaxation of the literal §19.6 rule in a direction the owner's note invites; revert by restoring the previous loop in `evaluateProposal` if not wanted.
**Rebuilding trades** (owner sells veterans, buys prospects, trades picks): sells are unrestricted by the gap; picks for spares and prospects work; the 8 proposals a window and 3 per club remain the pacing limit (unchanged).
**Rookie round trip (`roundtrip-seed51.txt`, `-53`).** The Rookie pick-shopping generosity (x1.35, unchanged since v42) is an intentional difficulty benefit. The only new leg in Task 5 is selling a monster back for picks. Measured round trip (shop every pick of rounds 1–3 for every position, take the best offer, lift the flip lock to model the next offseason, sell to the best club at the desk): Rookie recovers a median 0.35–0.46 of the pick value it started with, at most 1.00, none above it; Pro 0.24–0.35 (max 0.63); All-Star 0–0.18 (max 0.47). **No exploit**; the lock plus the desk margin and roster-fit pricing remove the spread. Not tested: many seasons of compounding (each leg loses value, so it cannot compound upward).

## 5. The trade deadline
**Before:** no deadline. Window and calls each had their own literal "weeks 2–8", a waiting offer was checked by its week stamp only, nothing told the player whether trading was open. The owner's own desk was already closed in season and open on the draft clock and in the offseason (approved).
**Rule implemented (GDD §54.10; `src/franchise/trade-window.js`, one function read by everything):** the regular season has 10 weeks. Trades are processed before the games of weeks 2–8; **the deadline is the end of week 8**. Weeks 9–10 and the postseason are closed. Everything reopens when the season ends: the draft clock and the offseason. Week 8 matches the existing weeks 2–8 and the 10-week schedule, so no conflict and **no change to the simulation**: same seeds give identical deals and state (checked before and after for 3 seeds x 3 seasons), so no matched-seed rerun is needed.
**Enforcement:** AI–AI trades (no random draw after week 8); incoming offers (none made after week 8, shown only while the window is open, refused and cleared at answer time even with a forged or edited save); the owner's proposals and counters (window check; no counter in a closed window); old pick-shopping uses the same desk window. Free agency untouched. **UI:** HQ shows a one-line calendar in season; the desk shows a banner (green open / red closed with why and when it reopens) and cannot send while closed; an offer states its deadline.
**Tests** (`tests/trading-second-pass.test.js`): the calendar table for weeks 1, 2, 7, 8, 9, 10, postseason and the offseason; AI deals in week 8 and none in 9, 10, 11; offers made and answered in week 8, expired in 9, forged stamps for 9/10/11 refused, cleared and reload-proof; owner proposals and counters blocked all season and in each week, reopened in the offseason whatever week the season ended; draft clock opens the desk; free agency works after the deadline; protections unchanged; determinism. Browser (`tools/trade-deadline-check.py`, 320 and 390 px, 7 saves from week 1 to the offseason): no problems, no errors.
